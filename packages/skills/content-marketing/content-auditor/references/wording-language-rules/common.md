# Wording rules - common to everything

These apply to every article, in every locale, on every topic. The auditor checks against them; the writer follows them.

Load order: this file first, then `locales/common.md` + `locales/<locale>.md`, then `topics/common.md` + `topics/<topic>.md`. A more specific file overrides a general one on conflict.

## Structure

- One idea per paragraph. Three to four sentences at most.
- Every H2 answers a real question a reader would ask.
- Front-load the answer. The first 100 words address the search intent, not throat-clearing.
- Use a list or table when it beats prose.

## Sentences

- Most sentences under 25 words.
- Active voice by default.
- Cut filler openers ("In today's fast-paced world", "It is important to note that").

## Honesty

- Every factual or statistical claim names a source.
- No invented numbers, quotes, or studies.
- Do not promise outcomes the article cannot back up.

## Keyword

- The keyword sits in the title, the first paragraph, and at least one H2.
- Never stuff it. If a sentence reads worse with the keyword, leave it out.

## AI-trace marks (remove all)

- No em dashes. Use a comma, a period, or parentheses.
- No smart/curly quotes or apostrophes. Use straight `"` and `'`.
- No decorative Unicode (arrows, bullets pasted mid-sentence).
